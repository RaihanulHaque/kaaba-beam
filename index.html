What is modelled
  • Orbit: JPL Keplerian elements of the Earth–Moon barycentre, with the slow inclination drift. Earth itself circles that point, pulled by the Moon.
  • Moon: lunar theory with the main 32 longitude, 14 latitude and distance terms (about 0.1° accuracy), its 5° tilted and 27.3-day orbit, near side always facing Earth, and phases from real sunlight. Moon altitude includes parallax from your site.
  • Orientation: sidereal time, IAU 1976 precession and 4-term nutation, so the axis moves against the stars.
  • Shape: WGS84 ellipsoid. The beam leaves along the ellipsoid normal at the site's altitude.
  • Stars: J2000 positions, shown on a far sphere. Read the angles, not the sphere's distance.
  • Left out: aberration (up to 20.5″), polar motion and UT1−UTC (about 13″), deflection of the vertical, star motion.
  • Beam direction should be good to a few arcseconds. Exaggerated sizes, the Moon's distance and the orbit shape are display choices; Kaaba sky view uses true sizes.
